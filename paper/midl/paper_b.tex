\begin{abstract}
Domain-adversarial training (DANN) is a standard remedy for site and device shortcuts
in medical imaging, and deployed models increasingly rely on out-of-distribution (OOD)
monitors to flag unfamiliar inputs. We show that the two can conflict silently. On
skin-lesion classification with a lesion-disjoint ISIC~2019 split, gradient reversal
toward smartphone images drives Mahalanobis OOD detection from AUROC $0.825$ to $0.381$
($95\%$ CI $[0.358, 0.404]$), and below chance on three clinical datasets no part of training
saw (Fitzpatrick17k $0.405$; MED-NODE and SD-198 \pending{last-checkpoint values}), while
in-distribution balanced accuracy stays at $0.47$--$0.48$ \pending{paired CI, calibration}. The
inversion is caused by label shift between the domains the adversary aligns: changing
only the class mix the adversary sees moves unseen-domain AUROC by $0.24$--$0.37$, and the
effect persists at matched domain leakage. Mechanistically, DANN pulls unfamiliar images
toward the majority class; under label shift it reaches chance-level unweighted domain
leakage by misaligning classes, while the domain stays decodable within each class.
The consequence is clinical: the share of melanomas from unseen sources predicted
benign nevus with confidence above $0.9$ rises from $8\%$ to $49$--$81\%$. Importance-weighted
alignment helps in proportion to how well the target class priors are known: with
label-free BBSE estimates it recovers $32$--$45\%$ of the lost AUROC at matched leakage,
and with priors from $50$ labelled target images detection returns to its pre-adaptation
level, at a cost of $0.08$ in-distribution balanced accuracy. OOD monitoring should be
re-validated after domain adaptation, and domain leakage should be measured within class.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Skin lesion classifiers learn acquisition artefacts as readily as pathology: ink
markings, rulers, dermatoscope type and contributing site all correlate with diagnosis
in public datasets~\cite{winkler2019,bissoto2020,daneshjou2022}. The standard remedy is to
make the representation invariant to the acquisition domain, most often by
domain-adversarial training with a gradient reversal layer~\cite{ganin2016,bousmalis2016}.
A deployed model also needs a second safeguard: an out-of-distribution (OOD) monitor that
flags inputs unlike its training data so that a clinician reviews them. This paper asks
what invariance training does to that monitor.

In our setting it can turn the monitor upside down. As the adversarial weight grows,
OOD detection does not merely weaken; it falls below chance, so that images from clinics
the model has never seen score as \emph{more} familiar than its own test data. Nothing in a
standard evaluation reveals this: in-distribution accuracy and calibration stay flat,
and the domain-leakage probe, the quantity the method is meant to reduce, improves. The
cost falls on the patients a monitor exists to protect: under the same training,
melanomas from unseen clinics are predicted benign nevus with confidence above $0.9$ up
to ten times more often, and the OOD monitor that should route them to a clinician ranks
them as routine.

We trace the failure to label shift. ISIC~2019 is $50.8\%$ nevus, the smartphone images the
adversary aligns against are $12.3\%$ nevus, and matching the marginal feature
distributions of domains with different class priors forces the representation to
misplace one domain's classes~\cite{zhao2019invariant,tachet2020domain}. We show the
consequence for OOD monitoring with an intervention that changes only the class mix the
adversary sees, and we locate the mechanism in the feature space.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The failure.} Under a lesion-disjoint split, gradient reversal inverts
Mahalanobis OOD detection on held-out smartphone images and on three clinical datasets
that no branch of training saw, with flat in-distribution accuracy and calibration,
under two training recipes whose accuracy is within $0.06$ of a standard ERM baseline on
the same split.
\item \textbf{The cause.} Setting the class mix seen by the adversary to that of the target
removes the inversion on unseen domains (AUROC difference $0.24$--$0.37$); a regression at
matched domain leakage attributes the effect to label shift rather than to deeper
invariance.
\item \textbf{The mechanism and a measurement lesson.} DANN pulls images that differ from
the source along the domain direction toward the majority class. Under label shift, an
unweighted domain probe reports chance-level leakage while a within-class probe still
reads the domain: invariance should be measured within class.
\item \textbf{Consequence and mitigation.} Melanomas from unseen sources are called benign
nevi with high confidence up to ten times more often. Importance weighting with label-free
prior estimates recovers part of the loss; with priors from $50$ labelled target images it
recovers fully, at a cost in in-distribution accuracy.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Shortcuts and invariance in dermatology.}
Ink markings shift melanoma predictions~\cite{winkler2019}, classifiers keep competitive
ISIC accuracy with the lesion masked out~\cite{bissoto2020}, and performance degrades on
darker skin~\cite{daneshjou2022}. Domain-adversarial training~\cite{ganin2016} and domain
separation networks~\cite{bousmalis2016} are the usual remedies; our dual-encoder model is
a domain separation network and is a vehicle for the intervention, not a contribution.

\paragraph{Invariance under label shift.}
When class priors differ between domains, matching marginal feature distributions cannot
also keep the joint error small~\cite{zhao2019invariant}; importance-weighted alignment
(IWDAN, IWCDAN) corrects for this with estimated target class priors~\cite{tachet2020domain},
which can be estimated without target labels by black-box shift estimation~\cite{lipton2018bbse}
or EM~\cite{saerens2002}. These results concern classification error. We study what the same
misalignment does to OOD monitoring, and show that a standard domain-leakage probe
misreports invariance under label shift.

\paragraph{OOD detection after adaptation.}
Full-spectrum OOD detection~\cite{yang2023,zhang2023} asks that covariate-shifted
in-distribution inputs be accepted; our smartphone and clinical images fall in that cell,
and Section~\ref{sec:tolerance} argues that acceptance is safe only when the model also
classifies them. Calibration degrades under shift~\cite{ovadia2019} and after domain
adaptation~\cite{wang2020}. Closest to our observation, Wu et al.\ report Mahalanobis and
ODIN AUROC below $0.5$ on rotated MNIST after adaptation with an MMD-based method
(DAN)~\cite{wu2023meta}, as a baseline in a study of continually adaptive detection. We
show the inversion on medical images, identify label shift as its cause, and characterise
the mechanism.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Data and splits}

\textbf{ISIC~2019}~\cite{isic2019} (25{,}331 dermoscopic images, eight classes: MEL, NV,
BCC, AK, BKL, DF, VASC, SCC) is the in-distribution source. We split it $60/20/20$ into
train, validation and test \emph{grouped by} \texttt{lesion\_id} (16{,}211 / 4{,}053 /
5{,}067 images), because $60\%$ of test images in the image-level split share a lesion with
training~\cite{leakagepaper}. \textbf{PAD-UFES-20}~\cite{pacheco2020} (2{,}298 smartphone
photographs, six of the eight classes) is the domain the adversary aligns against. Its
labels are never used in any loss. We split it by patient into \texttt{pad\_adv} (1{,}582
images, 961 patients), seen by the adversarial and context branches, and
\texttt{pad\_heldout} (716 images, 412 patients), never seen.

\textbf{Unseen domains.} Four clinical-photograph datasets enter no part of training:
Fitzpatrick17k~\cite{groh2021} (3{,}887 images obtained), MED-NODE (170 images),
SD-198 (6{,}584 images) and SCIN (10{,}406 images, 5{,}033 cases)
\pending{cite MED-NODE, SD-198, SCIN; licences: SCIN CC~BY~4.0, MED-NODE none stated, SD-198 official source}.
SCIN is mostly inflammatory conditions; only $0.6\%$ of its images map to the ISIC classes,
so we use it only as an OOD set for detection and report it separately; no label-based
analysis is done on SCIN. Images are cropped to a lesion-centred box and resized to
$224\times224$.

\subsection{Model and training}

A dual-encoder model with EfficientNet-B3 backbones produces a lesion latent
$z_\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{16}$ (batch-normalised, $z_\ell^{\mathrm{norm}}$) used for diagnosis
through a linear head, and a context latent $z_c\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{ctx}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ctx}}
+\lam\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{orth}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orth}},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ on ISIC only, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ a domain
cross-entropy on $z_c$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}$ a domain cross-entropy on
$z_\ell^{\mathrm{norm}}$ behind a gradient reversal layer, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orth}}$
a squared-cosine penalty between the latents. AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$; adversary
$\times30$, classifier $\times0.2$), 40 epochs, domain-balanced batches. We sweep
$\lam\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$ with everything else fixed (seeds 42--82 at $\lam\in\{0,2\}$,
42--46 elsewhere). A second recipe (A4: $z_\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$, classifier
$\times1$, class-weighted loss) serves as a robustness check.

\paragraph{Checkpoints.} We report the last checkpoint. Validation balanced accuracy (eight
classes, chance $0.125$) has a lesion-clustered bootstrap SE of $0.023$--$0.026$, and the
best-validation and last checkpoints differ by less than one SE, so best-validation selection
picks early epochs by noise. Best-validation values are given for comparison.

\paragraph{Competence.} In-distribution balanced accuracy at $\lam=0$ is $0.496$ (main
recipe) and $0.520$ (A4), $0.04$--$0.06$ below a standard single-encoder ERM on the same
split ($0.556$ \pending{ERM reference at 5 seeds, with SE}) and four times chance ($0.125$).
Lesion-disjoint eight-class ISIC~2019 is hard; the image-level split inflates the same
model's balanced accuracy by $0.20$~\cite{leakagepaper}.

\subsection{Interventions on label shift}

\textbf{Class mix seen by the adversary.} Keeping the classification loss on all of ISIC,
we resample only the ISIC images fed to the adversarial branch so that their class mix is
ISIC's own ($100\%$, the default), halfway to PAD's ($50\%$), or PAD's ($0\%$, no label
shift between the aligned domains), at $\lam\in\{0.5,2\}$ with five seeds (three at $50\%$).

\textbf{Importance-weighted alignment.} IWDAN and IWCDAN~\cite{tachet2020domain} reweight
the source in the adversarial loss by estimated target class priors; we compare BBSE
estimates~\cite{lipton2018bbse}, EM with temperature-scaled probabilities~\cite{saerens2002},
priors from $50$ labelled \texttt{pad\_adv} images (a few-label setting), and the true
\texttt{pad\_adv} priors (oracle, not deployable). CDAN~\cite{long2018cdan} is a
class-conditional adversarial baseline.

\subsection{Measurements}

\paragraph{OOD detection.} Class-conditional Mahalanobis with shared
covariance~\cite{lee2018} (primary), $k$-NN~\cite{sun2022}, maximum cosine to a class
centroid, maximum softmax probability~\cite{hendrycks2017} and energy~\cite{liu2020}, all
fit on ISIC train. AUROC treats OOD as the positive class, so $0.5$ is chance and values
below $0.5$ mean OOD images score as more familiar than ID test images. The five scores
are functions of the same latent, so they are not independent evidence.

\paragraph{Inversion.} We call a cell inverted when the cluster-bootstrap $95\%$ CI of the
seed-mean Mahalanobis AUROC lies below $0.5$ and at least three seeds' own intervals do
(all three in cells with three seeds). Bootstrap draws ($2{,}000$) resample ISIC test by lesion and OOD sets by
patient (\texttt{pad\_heldout}), near-duplicate cluster (Fitzpatrick17k) or case (SCIN);
MED-NODE and SD-198 carry no patient identifiers and are resampled by image, so their
intervals may be too narrow. Runs with in-distribution
balanced accuracy below $0.25$ are excluded as degraded and reported.

\paragraph{Domain leakage.} Balanced accuracy (chance $0.5$) of a logistic probe for the
source dataset on frozen $z_\ell^{\mathrm{norm}}$. The probe used in earlier work scores
ISIC test against all of PAD, including images the adversary trained on. We therefore also
report probes on held-out images only (linear, one-hidden-layer MLP, $k$-NN), a
class-balanced probe that reweights both domains to a common class mix, and a within-class
probe averaged over classes.

\paragraph{Calibration and diagnosis.} Expected calibration error (15 bins) on ISIC test and
on \texttt{pad\_heldout} (six shared classes), and zero-shot balanced accuracy on
\texttt{pad\_heldout}. Out-of-domain ECE is close to mean confidence minus accuracy here, so
these are views of one phenomenon.

\subsection{Label shift between the aligned domains causes the inversion}
\label{sec:site}

\paragraph{Intervention.} Changing only the class mix of the ISIC images the adversary sees
switches the inversion on unseen domains on and off (Table~\ref{tab:shift}). At $\lam=2$, with
the adversary's ISIC images resampled to PAD's class mix (shift $0\%$), Mahalanobis AUROC is
$0.691$ on Fitzpatrick17k, $0.541$ on MED-NODE and $0.694$ on SD-198, none inverted; with
ISIC's own mix (shift $100\%$) it is $0.340$, $0.298$ and $0.325$, inverted in every seed.
Paired by seed, removing the label shift raises AUROC by $0.361$, $0.243$ and $0.372$. The
intermediate level falls between the two (Fitzpatrick17k $0.482$ at $\lam=0.5$, $0.441$ at
$\lam=2$). On \texttt{pad\_heldout}, the adversary's own target domain, a mild inversion
remains without label shift ($0.458$; paired difference $+0.093$). One of five shift-$0\%$
runs collapsed (in-distribution balanced accuracy $0.139$) and is excluded.

\paragraph{Matched leakage.} Removing label shift also leaves more domain information in the
latent (leakage $0.791$ vs $0.634$), so the intervention alone does not separate label shift
from depth of invariance. A regression of unseen-domain AUROC on label-shift level and
leakage, with recipe as a covariate (variance inflation $\approx1.04$), gives label-shift
coefficients of $-0.166$ $[-0.217, -0.115]$ (Fitzpatrick17k), $-0.107$ $[-0.148, -0.065]$
(MED-NODE) and $-0.172$ $[-0.221, -0.123]$ (SD-198) for a change from no shift to full shift
at fixed leakage \pending{confirm shift coding and resampling unit}. On \texttt{pad\_heldout}
the coefficient is $+0.041$ $[-0.033, +0.110]$. Comparing runs at equal leakage across recipes
(A4 at shift $100\%$, leakage $0.78$, against the main recipe at shift $0\%$, leakage $0.79$)
gives the same direction ($+0.23$ to $+0.26$).

\paragraph{A natural example without DANN.} Label shift alone can invert a class-conditional
Mahalanobis score. Training on BCN and treating HAM as OOD, Mahalanobis AUROC is already
$0.369$ $[0.336, 0.401]$ at $\lam=0$, while $k$-NN gives $0.658$. HAM-heldout is $67\%$ nevus
against $31\%$ in BCN test; reweighting HAM to BCN's class mix gives $0.489$ $[0.453, 0.524]$,
and no class-matched AUROC differs from $0.5$ (post hoc). In the main setting the OOD sets
are \emph{less} nevus-heavy than ISIC, so detection works at $\lam=0$; DANN creates the
condition by moving them toward nevus.

\subsection{Mechanism: misaligned classes behind chance-level leakage}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\label{sec:nonmediation}

\paragraph{Unfamiliar images move to the majority class.} At $\lam=2$ with full label shift,
the share of \texttt{pad\_heldout} images whose nearest class centroid is nevus rises from
$0.28$ to $0.63$, and to $0.75$--$0.85$ on the unseen sets; without label shift it stays at
$0.12$ and $0.39$--$0.49$. Images of non-nevus classes are pulled into the nevus region:
split by true class, non-nevus OOD images fall to AUROC $0.42$ (main recipe) and $0.47$ (A4),
whereas images still nearest a non-nevus centroid stay at $0.62$ and $0.64$. Because pooled
AUROC is the class-share-weighted mean of per-class AUROCs, and nevus is half the ISIC test
set, this displacement is enough to push the pooled score below chance.

\paragraph{The displacement follows the domain direction.} For each OOD image we measured
how far it lies along the direction separating ISIC from PAD in the $\lam=0$ model, and how
much closer to its nearest ID centroid it moves at $\lam=2$. The two correlate on every
lesion dataset (Spearman $0.33$--$0.47$), while the deviation orthogonal to that direction
does not predict the pull ($|\rho|\le0.07$ on four of five sets). Between two $\lam=0$ models
of different seeds the same correlation is smaller ($0.11$--$0.29$); the DANN-specific
excess exceeds $0.15$ on all four unseen datasets ($0.18$--$0.33$) but not on
\texttt{pad\_heldout} ($0.057$), and is larger in the pre-projection backbone ($0.24$--$0.45$
on all five).

\paragraph{Chance-level leakage hides the misalignment.} The domain probe of
Table~\ref{tab:dial} is scored partly on images the adversary trained on. On held-out images
a linear probe reads the domain at chance from $\lam=0.5$ on ($0.502$--$0.506$), but a $k$-NN
probe still reads it ($0.55$--$0.61$), and under label shift an unweighted probe misreports
invariance (Table~\ref{tab:probes}). With full label shift DANN reaches $0.510$ on an
unweighted held-out probe, yet within each class the domain remains decodable ($0.618$),
more so than in any other condition. Correcting the class mix the adversary aligns (shift
$0\%$, or importance weighting with the true priors) raises the unweighted reading
($0.63$--$0.64$) while lowering the within-class one ($0.56$--$0.57$). Under label shift,
DANN matches the marginal distributions by misaligning classes, as the theory of
\citet{zhao2019invariant} implies; leakage should therefore be measured within class
(post hoc).

\subsection{Clinical consequence and mitigation}
\label{sec:confidently}
\label{sec:adversarial}

\paragraph{Melanomas called benign.} With a threshold accepting $95\%$ of ISIC validation
images, almost all unseen-domain malignant images are accepted at every $\lam$, so the
monitor does not catch them in either case. What changes is the classifier: the share of
melanomas predicted nevus with confidence above $0.9$ rises from $7.5\%$ to $48.7\%$ on
Fitzpatrick17k and from $8.1\%$ to $81.2\%$ on SD-198 between $\lam=0$ and $\lam=2$
\pending{n and CIs per cell; label map}.

\paragraph{No fix at scoring time.} Reversing the sign of the score is not a remedy: at
$\lam\ge0.5$ it reaches only $0.55$--$0.63$ on \texttt{pad\_heldout} and Fitzpatrick17k with a
false-positive rate of $0.84$--$0.95$ at $95\%$ true-positive rate \pending{verify FPR range in s\_flip}, and at $\lam=0$ it would
turn $0.808$ into $0.192$. Relative Mahalanobis~\cite{ren2021relative} and per-class
standardised distances stay below $0.5$ on all three unseen datasets at $\lam=2$.

\paragraph{Importance-weighted alignment.} IWDAN reweights the source in the adversarial loss
by estimated target class priors, and how well it works depends on the estimate.
\emph{BBSE} priors are far from the truth (total variation $0.65$--$0.76$), plausibly because
its assumption of unchanged class-conditional inputs fails across these domains; IWDAN then
keeps leakage at the DANN level ($0.642$ vs $0.634$) and recovers $32\%$ of the lost AUROC on
\texttt{pad\_heldout} ($0.513$) and $45\%$ on Fitzpatrick17k ($0.508$), while MED-NODE
($0.350$) and SD-198 ($0.488$) stay at or below chance. \emph{EM} estimation was unstable:
in two of five seeds it placed mass on classes absent from PAD, so we treat it as not
having run correctly. With priors from \emph{$50$ labelled target images} (total variation
$0.10$--$0.14$), detection on the unseen domains returns to the level obtained with the true
priors (Fitzpatrick17k $0.741$ vs $0.717$, SD-198 $0.734$ vs $0.693$, MED-NODE $0.606$ vs $0.563$;
\texttt{pad\_heldout} $0.619$ vs $0.647$), and within-class leakage is lower than under DANN
($0.58$ vs $0.62$). Neither the 50-label nor the true-prior variant meets our condition for a
fix, because unweighted leakage rises ($0.81$ and $0.78$ against $0.63$), and both cost
in-distribution balanced accuracy ($0.39$--$0.40$ against $0.48$). Across estimators, the
error in the estimated priors did not predict detection as a rank correlation (Spearman
$-0.33$, $[-0.80, +0.28]$), with the EM runs contributing invalid priors
\pending{IWCDAN, CDAN, $\lam=0.5$ cells; per-method details to appendix}.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{An inversion, not a degradation.}
The central result is not that out-of-distribution detection weakens under adversarial
invariance training but that the ordering reverses. Degradation would be unsurprising
--- a representation stripped of domain information should be worse at recognising a
domain, and chance is what that argument predicts, which is what a single-encoder model
reaches at $\lam = 0.25$ ($0.525$). Going \emph{past} chance is not predicted by it,
and is what Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} accounts for. The results then compose into one
failure sequence: a model trained with invariance to acquisition domain, as
recommended, is deployed at a new site, where it increasingly predicts the benign
majority class --- actinic keratosis recall $0.245 \to 0.016$ --- with higher
confidence than on its own test data, while the monitor that should flag those images
ranks them as more routine than familiar ones and every metric the developer watches
moves favourably.

\paragraph{The failure belongs to gradient reversal, not to invariance.}
Reducing leakage is not sufficient to produce it, and in this sweep leakage is not even
achieved: it bottoms out at $0.555$ against a floor of $0.5$ and rises again at the
largest weights while detection stays inverted. MMD reduces leakage along the same axis
and collapses nothing at the levels it reaches with the classifier intact, where
gradient reversal has already inverted. The two also fail in opposite manners: pushed
harder, MMD becomes less accurate \emph{and} less confident and its out-of-domain
calibration error falls, while under gradient reversal accuracy falls as confidence
rises and calibration error triples. One degrades honestly, the other is confidently
wrong. Wald et al.~\cite{wald2021} showed that enforcing multi-domain calibration
\emph{produces} invariance; the converse fails here, which suggests that where
invariance is the goal, calibration-based or moment-matching objectives may be
preferable because they leave the out-of-domain uncertainty signal intact. We offer
that as a hypothesis: MMD never reached the invariance level gradient reversal reaches
without destroying the task, and we tested one adversarial formulation, so whether CDAN
or adversarial discriminative adaptation behaves the same way is not established here.

\paragraph{The danger zone is where the method succeeds.}
Risk scales with \emph{achieved} invariance, not with the nominal weight or the
presence of an adversarial term. Where the adversary never learned, leakage stayed at
$0.94$ across four orders of magnitude of $\lam$ and the safety failure did not appear
either. A weak adversary produces neither debiasing nor harm --- so many deployed
configurations may be harmless, and a configuration's safety cannot be read off its
hyperparameters.

\paragraph{Tolerance without generalisation.}\label{sec:tolerance}
Full-spectrum OOD detection \cite{yang2023} prescribes that covariate-shifted
in-distribution inputs be accepted rather than flagged. Our models achieve that
prescription and are unsafe because of it: they accept out-of-domain images more
readily than their own test data while classifying them at $0.254$ balanced accuracy
against a floor of $0.167$. Acceptance and competence are separate properties, and
current protocols evaluate them separately or not at all. Any claim of tolerance to
covariate shift should carry cross-domain accuracy on the same inputs, since tolerating
inputs a model cannot classify is a failure mode rather than a success.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
Report OOD detection and out-of-domain calibration \textbf{as a function of achieved
invariance} rather than at a single operating point; report cross-domain accuracy
alongside any claim of tolerance to covariate shift; and state, when a separate
dataset serves as the OOD set, that this measures covariate shift rather than
semantic novelty. Practitioners tuning $\lam$ already train at several values, so
this changes what is reported rather than what is run. It converts an invisible
failure into a visible number.

\paragraph{Monitor outside the adversarial path.}
Since the collapse is a property of the representation that received adversarial
gradient, one mitigation is to score novelty on a representation that did not. A
frozen ImageNet ResNet-50 gives Mahalanobis AUROC $0.988$ on Fitzpatrick17k and
$0.833$ on \texttt{pad\_heldout}, against the trained model's $0.764$ and $0.843$ at
$\lam = 0$ and $0.391$ and $0.427$ at $\lam = 2$. It never inverts, but \textbf{we
had pre-registered $0.90$ in all four cells and \texttt{pad\_heldout} does not reach
it}, where the frozen encoder merely matches the untrained model rather than
improving on it. A partial mitigation, not a fix; retreating to an earlier layer of
the \emph{same} encoder does not work at all (Section~\ref{sec:nonmediation}).

\paragraph{The inversion is deepened, not created, in the minority classes.}
\label{sec:perclass}
Per-class AUROC, which unlike the pooled value does not depend on the ID class mix,
is $0.14$--$0.39$ on \texttt{pad\_heldout} and $0.10$--$0.34$ on Fitzpatrick17k at
$\lam = 2$ for every non-nevus class. Two are already at or below chance beforehand: on
\texttt{pad\_heldout} at $\lam = 0$, actinic keratosis is $0.499$ and squamous cell
carcinoma $0.444$.
Gradient reversal deepens an inversion that already exists for the rarest and most
clinically consequential classes, and the pooled AUROC at $\lam = 0$ conceals this
because nevus is half the in-distribution set.

\paragraph{The mechanism, and what remains open.}
Gradient reversal removes the acquisition signal by moving out-of-domain images into
the region the majority class occupies (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}) --- an efficient
way to satisfy the adversary, since the densest part of the latent space is where
domain information is hardest to recover, and simultaneously the worst outcome for a
distance-based novelty score, which reads density as familiarity. The two objectives
are not merely in tension: invariance is obtained by the move that defeats detection.
What remains open is why gradient reversal takes this route and moment matching does
not, why the synthetic reproduction failed, and whether the majority class is special
or merely the densest available --- a dataset with two comparably sized dominant
classes would separate the last of these.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Absolute cross-domain performance is poor for every model here.} Plain
accuracy on \texttt{pad\_heldout} is $0.245$ for the factorized model and $0.391$ for
the baseline against a $0.385$ majority rate, so no model here is deployable. Our
claim concerns the \emph{effect of $\lam$}, and the deployment pathway above is a
mechanism of harm rather than a description of a fielded system.

\textbf{The inversion is observed only across imaging modality.} ISIC is dermoscopy
while PAD-UFES and Fitzpatrick17k are clinical photography, so the main contrast
confounds site with modality and Fitzpatrick17k does not resolve it. The
same-modality contrast within ISIC (Section~\ref{sec:site}) separates the claims: the
collapse reproduces between two hospitals, the inversion does not. We cannot say
whether that reflects the size of the shift, modality gaps specifically, or these
datasets.

Three attempts to replicate outside dermatology returned no result, for the same
underlying reason in different guises: the manipulation did not take, so there was
nothing to measure. On three-hospital Camelyon17 the adversary head never trained; on
iWildCam it trained but the encoder never became invariant; and on Camelyon17
restricted to \emph{two} hospitals --- the obvious diagnosis, matching the dermatology
domain count, with the gradient path verified first --- the adversary reached chance
($\ln 2$) while a post-hoc probe still read the hospital at $0.92$--$0.95$, a fall of
at most $0.037$ against the $0.10$ we had pre-committed. The pre-registered stopping
rule returns a negative and we stop. \textbf{Domain count is not the barrier}, and we
cannot say what is; the pattern --- an adversary driven to chance while the information
it was asked to remove stays linearly decodable --- is a known failure mode of
adversarial removal \cite{elazar2018}. \textbf{We report no inversion outside
dermatology}; a single-seed sub-chance reading there did not replicate
($0.534 \pm 0.050$ over three seeds). What those runs do support is the dissociation in
Section~\ref{sec:nonmediation}. Details in Appendix~\ref{app:external}.

Generality beyond dermatology remains untested. \textbf{Backbone coverage.} Comparable invariance was not reached with a ResNet-50
backbone, so whether the inversion extends beyond EfficientNet-B3 is untested. Three of
the nine runs failed their adversary gate outright and are reported as null experiments
rather than negative results. \textbf{The objective comparison is bounded.} MMD never reached the invariance
level gradient reversal attains; it could only approach it by destroying the
classifier. We therefore cannot say what moment-matching would do at matched
invariance, only that at the leakage levels it reaches, gradient reversal has
already inverted and MMD has not.

\textbf{Detector non-independence.} The five scoring rules are functions of one
16-dimensional latent; the backbone analysis and the single-encoder replication carry
the independent evidence. \textbf{Calibration arithmetic.} Out-of-domain ECE is close
to mean confidence minus accuracy here, so those three results are one phenomenon
viewed three ways. \textbf{Seeds.} Five at every $\lam$ on the leak-free split, and
three at two interior points of the image-level sweep in the appendix; effect sizes
are large relative to seed variance, but we claim no formal test across seven
points.

\textbf{Splits.} The main sweep uses the image-level ISIC split, in which
$3{,}041$ of $5{,}067$ test images ($60.0\%$) share a \texttt{lesion\_id} with
training or validation. This inflates in-distribution balanced accuracy by
$0.20$ ($0.496 \to 0.692$ at $\lam = 0$), so the absolute values in
Table~\ref{tab:dial} are optimistic by that amount;
Section~\ref{sec:lesionsplit} reports the lesion-disjoint replication at five
seeds, under which every conclusion is unchanged. PAD splits are patient-level,
disjoint by patient and by lesion. \textbf{Third-domain coverage.} 3{,}887 of 16{,}577 Fitzpatrick17k images were
obtained; the set is web-scraped under mixed copyright, used for evaluation only,
and not redistributed.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

Under domain-adversarial training, out-of-distribution detection does not merely
weaken. It reverses: a model asked which inputs are unfamiliar answers with its
own test data and ranks a new clinic's images as the more routine of the two.
The reversal appears at the smallest adversarial weight that achieves
invariance, holds on a dataset the adversary never saw, reproduces without the
factorized architecture, and does not appear under moment-matching objectives
that reduce leakage along the same axis.

It is invisible to everything a developer monitors. In-distribution accuracy
holds, in-distribution calibration is unchanged and the domain leakage probe
improves exactly as intended --- under an image-level and a lesion-disjoint ISIC
split alike --- while confidence on unseen domains overtakes confidence on
familiar data and recall for a premalignant class falls to $0.016$.

The cause is identifiable: invariance is obtained by moving out-of-domain
images into the majority class's region of the latent space, which is exactly
the move that makes a density-based novelty score call them familiar. Acting on
it does not require the explanation. Reporting out-of-distribution detection,
out-of-domain calibration and cross-domain accuracy as functions of achieved
invariance costs almost nothing and makes the failure visible.

\subsection{Confidence, accuracy and calibration on unseen domains}
\label{app:confident}

At $\lam = 0$ the model is appropriately hesitant on unfamiliar data: mean
maximum-softmax confidence is $0.915$ on the ISIC test split and $0.489$ on
\texttt{pad\_heldout}. By $\lam = 0.25$ that gap has closed ($0.913$ versus $0.898$),
and by $\lam = 2$ it has inverted ($0.930$ versus $0.910$).

Cross-domain diagnostic accuracy does not improve at any $\lam$: balanced accuracy
spans $0.249$--$0.291$ against a six-class floor of $0.167$, no setting exceeds the
$0.291$ obtained at $\lam = 0$, and every setting is below the $0.298$ of the
non-adversarial EfficientNet-B3 control. Plain accuracy falls $0.245 \to 0.184$,
beneath the $0.385$ majority-class rate throughout. Macro AUC is roughly preserved
($0.605 \to 0.581$), so discrimination survives while the decision rule does not.

Predictions collapse toward the benign majority class: nevus predictions rise from
$113$ to $374$ of $716$ images, and actinic keratosis recall falls from $0.245$ to
$0.016$ ($49/\nAK \to 3/\nAK$ images, mean over five seeds). Squamous cell carcinoma
moves the same way ($0.088 \to 0.021$, $6/\nSCC \to 1/\nSCC$) but the count is too
small to carry a claim on its own, and melanoma ($n = \nMEL$) supports none.

In this setting out-of-domain ECE is arithmetically close to mean confidence minus
accuracy, which is why the confidence, accuracy and calibration results are not
independent corroboration of each other.

\subsection{Single-encoder replication}
\label{app:single}

Plain single-encoder domain-adversarial training on the same data and splits. Every
detector collapses at $\lam = 0.25$ --- $k$-NN $0.944 \to 0.528$, energy
$0.911 \to 0.507$, cosine $0.884 \to 0.509$, MSP $0.868 \to 0.515$ --- and by
$\lam = 4$ four of the five sit below chance, cosine most clearly at
$0.454 \pm 0.014$. In-distribution accuracy is flat throughout ($0.845 \to 0.832$) and
cross-domain accuracy falls from $0.390$ to a plateau near $0.32$.

\subsection{External replication attempts}
\label{app:external}

\textbf{Camelyon17, three hospitals.} The adversary head never trained:
cross-entropy sat at $\ln 3$ from the first epoch at every $\lam$ while a post-hoc
probe on the same features read $0.94$, despite a gradient graph verified correct
(head cosine $+1.000$, encoder $-1.000$).

\textbf{iWildCam.} The adversary did train --- best-epoch cross-entropy $3.13$ against
$\ln 243 = 5.49$ --- but the encoder never became invariant: the binary
train-versus-OOD site probe moved only from $0.911$ to $0.891$ across
$\lam \in [0, 10]$ against a floor of $0.5$, and no detector collapsed.

\textbf{Camelyon17, two hospitals.} Pre-committed in
\texttt{PRECOMMIT\_CAMELYON2.json} before running. The adversary reached chance
(final cross-entropy $0.692$ against $\ln 2 = 0.693$, from $0.11$ at $\lam = 0$) and
in-distribution accuracy held at $0.995$, but the hospital probe fell only
$0.959 \pm 0.007 \to 0.952 \pm 0.001$, inside its own seed spread, so the leakage gate
failed and the verdict is negative. The exploratory seeds added afterwards do not
reopen it: Mahalanobis on the held-out hospital reads $0.534 \pm 0.050$ at $\lam = 1$
over seeds $42$--$44$, above chance, so the single-seed $0.478$ did not replicate.
These runs appear in Section~\ref{sec:nonmediation} for the non-mediation claim only,
where the five detectors move $0.12$--$0.22$ while the probe moves $0.007$.

\subsection{Same-modality site shift: probe validation and the \texttt{HAM} direction}
\label{app:site}

\textbf{The sites are separable, and not for trivial reasons.} A logistic probe on
frozen ERM features distinguishes \texttt{BCN} from \texttt{HAM} at balanced accuracy
$0.962 \pm 0.007$ against a floor of $0.5$. Masking the dermatoscope vignette reduces
this only to $0.943 \pm 0.005$, so the border is not the signal. Hand-crafted colour
and intensity statistics reach $0.901$ at best --- close to the $0.876$ ceiling the
same features reach on ISIC$\leftrightarrow$PAD, so this pair is no more low-level than
the original contrast --- while sharpness statistics sit at chance ($0.532$). A probe
given only the ground-truth class label reaches $0.668$, reflecting the two sites'
different class distributions; the feature probe is far above that reference.

\textbf{Mahalanobis is uninformative in the \texttt{BCN}-in-distribution direction.} It
reads $0.379$ at $\lam = 0$, already below chance before any adversarial training.
Reweighting the in-distribution set to the out-of-domain class mix raises it to
$0.487$, so most of that baseline inversion is class composition --- \texttt{HAM} is
two-thirds nevus and therefore concentrated near a single well-populated centroid ---
with a residual we attribute to HAM10000's more standardised acquisition and
correspondingly lower feature variance. This is why the reversed direction, which
restores headroom, carries the scope claim in Section~\ref{sec:site}. In that
direction the class-reweighted variant falls from $0.74$ to $0.54$, and Mahalanobis
reads $0.719 \pm 0.019$ at $\lam = 0.25$.

\subsection{A domain monitor is cheap, and semantic novelty is unaffected}

A linear domain head on a frozen ImageNet ResNet-50 that has never seen this data
reaches AUROC $0.998$; on the trained baseline, $0.997$; on the trained EfficientNet-B3
control, $0.991$. Detecting the acquisition domain is easy from almost any
representation, so retaining a monitor is not in itself difficult. What distinguishes
the dual-encoder's context branch is \emph{concentration}: a single principal component
carries $84.5\%$ of its variance and already separates the domains
(AUROC $>0.9999$, two discordant pairs in $11.6$M), against $0.55$--$0.70$ at $k = 1$
for every other representation tested.

The damage is specific to covariate shift. With the domain held constant and diagnostic
classes withheld from training instead, both branches land between $0.6$ and $0.8$ and
the ordering depends on which class is withheld (DF and VASC: lesion $0.648$, context
$0.641$, baseline $0.687$; SCC: lesion $0.806$, context $0.607$, baseline $0.684$). No
near/far structure organises the difference (Pearson $-0.12$ against a
centroid-similarity index). We report this as a null result.
